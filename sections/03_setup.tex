\section{Setup and notation}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Data.}
A pool of comparisons $X_1,\dots,X_N$, with $X_i=(x_i,y_{A,i},y_{B,i})$, is drawn from $P_X$. A frozen
judge is queried on every comparison, giving $\QJ(\cdot\mid X_i)$ for all $i\le N$. The dataset
supplied for this project already contains a human label $Y_i$ for every comparison. During
training, only the labels indexed by $\cI_n\subset[N]$, $|\cI_n|=n\ll N$, are revealed; the others are
held out for evaluation. \textbf{No new human annotation is collected.}

\paragraph{Human outcome and its embedding.}
Let $\cS_H$ be the human outcome space and $\QH^{*}(\cdot\mid X)$ the target conditional distribution.
We represent distributions through a feature map $\phi:\cS_H\to\cH$ and write $\cM$ for the closed
convex hull of $\phi(\cS_H)$. The conditional mean embedding $\E\{\phi(Y)\mid\cdot\}$ then always
lies in $\cM$.

\begin{center}
\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
Human label & $\phi(y)$ & $\E\{\phi(Y)\mid\cdot\}$ & $\norm{\cdot}^2$ risk \\
\midrule
Binary $\{0,1\}$ & $y$ & $p_H^{*}$ & Brier / MSE \\
Categorical $\{1..K\}$ & one-hot $e_y$ & probability vector & multiclass Brier \\
Ordinal $\{1..K\}$ & $(\ind\{y\le k\})_{k<K}$ & CDF vector & RPS (discrete CRPS) \\
General & kernel feature & conditional mean embedding & conditional MMD$^2$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent For the kernel row see \citet{song2009hilbert,muandet2017kernel}. If $r$ annotators label a
comparison, their averaged embedding is an unbiased label whose noise variance is divided by $r$.

\paragraph{Judge-side objects.}
The judge's support $\cS_J$ may differ from $\cS_H$. For example, the judge may give a five-point graded
preference while the human label is binary. A known \emph{link} $T$ maps judge distributions into $\cM$
and defines the judge's prediction
\[
  \mJ(X) \;=\; T\{\QJ(\cdot\mid X)\}\in\cM .
\]
If $\cS_J=\cS_H$, then $T(Q)=\E_{Z\sim Q}\phi(Z)$. For a graded judge and a binary human label, one
choice is $T(Q)=Q(A\gg B)+Q(A>B)+\tfrac12 Q(\text{tie})$. Position bias is handled by averaging the
judge over both presentation orders.

\paragraph{Localization variable.}
Kernel smoothing needs a low-dimensional covariate, and the raw text $X$ is not one. We localize in
\[
  Z=\zeta(X)=\bigl(\psi\{\QJ(\cdot\mid X)\},\,\xi(X)\bigr)\in\cZ\subset\R^{d}.
\]
Here $\psi$ is a low-dimensional summary of the judge distribution, for example the judge
probability $p_J$, tie mass, entropy, or dispersion across repeated samples. $\xi$ holds optional
coarse content features, such as task category, length difference, or a low-dimensional projection
of an embedding. We require $\mJ(X)$ to be a function of $Z$; this holds whenever $\psi$ determines
$T(\QJ)$. We then write $\mJ(z)$.

\paragraph{Estimand.}
\[
  \muH(z)=\E\{\phi(Y)\mid Z=z\}\in\cM .
\]
For binary labels, $\muH=p_H^{*}$. The estimand depends on $\zeta$: it is the per-comparison target
$\E\{\phi(Y)\mid X\}$ coarsened to $Z$. Section~\ref{sec:scalar-vs-dist} shows that this coarsening is
exactly where the full judge distribution can matter.

\keybox{\textbf{\textcolor{BrickRed}{Design decision D1: the localization variable $\zeta$.}}
Options: (a) $Z=\psi(\QJ)$ only, which is low-dimensional and lets the theory transfer directly;
(b) $\psi(\QJ)$ plus a few content features; (c) a high-dimensional embedding, which suffers from the
curse of dimensionality and would need an intrinsic-dimension analysis. The default is (a) or (b)
with $d\le 4$.}
