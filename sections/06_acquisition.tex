\section{Method II: Distribution-aware human-label acquisition}
\label{sec:acquisition}

In our retrospective experiments we choose which existing pool labels to reveal. Revealing a label
simulates annotating that comparison, so design can be studied without recruiting annotators. This
is the pool-based setting of Algorithm~4 in \citet{li2026personalizing}: the pool $\{Z_i\}_{i\le N}$,
with density $q$, is fully observed, and we choose $n$ points to label. The test set $\mathcal T$ is
fixed beforehand, so every design is evaluated on the same comparisons.

\subsection{Oracle design calculation}

Let $p$ denote the design density of revealed points. The risk $R$ integrates against the
population density $q$, not Lebesgue measure.

\paragraph{Global bandwidth.}
The design enters only through the variance term $\int q(z)\sigma^2(z)/\{np(z)h^d\}\,dz$, which is
minimized by
\[
  p^{*}\propto\sigma\sqrt q .
\]
The rule $p^{*}\propto\sigma$ of \citet{li2026personalizing} is the special case of Lebesgue risk, or
of uniform $q$. For binary labels $\sigma^2=p_H^{*}(1-p_H^{*})$, so labels go where \emph{humans} are
split. Judge uncertainty is not the criterion.

\paragraph{Local bandwidth.}
With a locally adaptive bandwidth $h(z)$, the pointwise risk is approximately
$\gamma_1(z)^2h^{2\gamma_2}+\sigma^2(z)/\{np(z)h^d\}$. Here $\gamma_1(z)$ is the local H\"older constant
of the discrepancy $\delta_{\theta,z}$ near $z$. Optimizing $h$ at each point gives
$A(z)\{np(z)\}^{-2\gamma_2/(2\gamma_2+d)}$, with $A=\gamma_1^{2d/(2\gamma_2+d)}\sigma^{4\gamma_2/(2\gamma_2+d)}$.
Minimizing $\int qA\,p^{-2\gamma_2/(2\gamma_2+d)}$ subject to $\int p=1$ gives
\begin{equation}
  p^{*}(z)\;\propto\;q(z)^{\frac{2\gamma_2+d}{4\gamma_2+d}}\;
  \gamma_1(z)^{\frac{2d}{4\gamma_2+d}}\;\sigma(z)^{\frac{4\gamma_2}{4\gamma_2+d}},
  \qquad
  \min_p\,n^{-\alpha}J(p)=n^{-\alpha}
  \Bigl(\int (qA)^{\frac{2\gamma_2+d}{4\gamma_2+d}}\Bigr)^{\frac{4\gamma_2+d}{2\gamma_2+d}},
  \label{eq:design}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha=2\gamma_2/(2\gamma_2+d)$ and $J(p)=\int qA\,p^{-\alpha}$. The quantity $n^{-\alpha}J(p)$ is
a \emph{surrogate upper bound} on the risk under design $p$, not the risk itself.
The rule combines three factors: where comparisons are \emph{common} ($q$), where humans are
\emph{split} ($\sigma$), and where the judge-to-human discrepancy \emph{varies} ($\gamma_1$). Setting
$q$ uniform and letting $d\to0$ recovers the $\sigma$-proportional rule. This is an oracle calculation:
$\gamma_1(\cdot)$, $\sigma(\cdot)$ and $q$ are unknown.

\keybox{\textbf{Design principle.} A constant offset between judge and humans is cheap to learn, even
when it is large. Labels should go where the discrepancy \emph{varies}, weighted by how often such
comparisons occur, not merely where the judge disagrees with humans or is unsure.}

\subsection{Plug-in algorithm (a heuristic until Target~\ref{tr:design} is proved)}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
  \item \emph{Pilot.} Reveal $n_0=cn$ pool labels uniformly at random. As in Algorithm~1 of
        \citet{li2026personalizing}, half go to pilot estimation and half to validation.
  \item \emph{Pilot fit.} Fit \dfsp{} on the pilot half, giving $\hat\theta$ and $\hat\mu_{\mathrm{pil}}$.
        Set $\hat\sigma^2(z)=\hat\mu_{\mathrm{pil}}(z)\{1-\hat\mu_{\mathrm{pil}}(z)\}$ for binary labels;
        otherwise use the kernel variance estimator of \citet{li2026personalizing}.
  \item \emph{Discrepancy complexity.} Let
        $\hat\delta_z(\cdot)=\hat\mu_{\mathrm{pil}}(\cdot)-\omega_{\hat\theta,z}\circ\mJ(\cdot)$ be the estimated
        center-dependent, truncated discrepancy. Define
        $\hat\gamma_1(z)=\max\{\epsilon,\ \max_{z'\in\mathcal N_\rho(z)}\norm{\hat\delta_z(z')-\hat\delta_z(z)}/\norm{z'-z}^{\hat\theta_2}\}$.
        Here $\mathcal N_\rho(z)$ is a grid on the annulus $\rho/2\le\norm{z'-z}\le\rho$, and
        $\gamma_2$ is fixed at $\hat\theta_2$. The scale $\rho$ must satisfy
        $\norm{\hat\mu_{\mathrm{pil}}-\muH}_\infty=o_P(\rho^{\gamma_2})$. Otherwise the difference
        quotient is dominated by noise: with $\rho=h_{\mathrm{pil}}\asymp m^{-1/3}$, $d=1$ and
        $\gamma_2=1$, its variance is of order $1/(mh^3)\asymp1$. If the pilot sup-norm error is
        $O_P(h_{\mathrm{pil}}^{\gamma_2}\log m)$, then $\rho=h_{\mathrm{pil}}^{1/2}$ suffices. We do not
        claim adaptation to $\gamma_2$.
  \item \emph{Target design.} Estimate $q$ by a kernel density estimate $\hat q$ on the pool, which
        is fully observed. Plug $\hat q,\hat\gamma_1,\hat\sigma$ into \eqref{eq:design} to get
        $\hat p$, then mix: $\tilde p=(1-\eta)\hat p+\eta\hat q$ with $\eta\in(0,1)$. The mixture keeps
        $\tilde p\ge\eta\hat q$, i.e.\ overlap with the pool.
  \item \emph{Pool sampling.} Draw the remaining $n-n_0$ labels from the pool without replacement,
        with probabilities proportional to $\tilde p(Z_i)/\hat q(Z_i)$.
  \item \emph{Local bandwidth.} Fit \dfsp{} with the plug-in local bandwidth
        \[
          \hat h(z)=\min\Bigl\{\Bigl(\frac{\hat\sigma(z)}{\hat\gamma_1(z)}\Bigr)^{\frac{2}{2\gamma_2+d}}
          \{n\tilde p(z)\}^{-\frac{1}{2\gamma_2+d}},\,1\Bigr\},
        \]
        floored so that $n\tilde p(z)\hat h(z)^d\ge C\log n$. This is the pointwise optimizer of the
        surrogate.
\end{enumerate}
The validation set comes from the uniform pilot, so the adaptation step of Section~\ref{sec:dfsp}
needs no importance weighting.

\paragraph{What the calculation does and does not give.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:design} minimizes a risk \emph{upper bound} built from a H\"older bias envelope. The
bound need not be tight: a locally linear discrepancy has zero interior bias under symmetric
averaging, even though its local Lipschitz constant is positive. So minimizing the envelope does not
prove that actual risk improves. By \citet{castro2005faster}, no design, active or passive, changes
the \emph{rate} for H\"older classes. Method~II is about constants.

\begin{target}[Conditional guarantee for the mixed plug-in design]
\label{tr:design}
Let $J(p)=\int qA\,p^{-\alpha}$ with $\alpha=2\gamma_2/(2\gamma_2+d)$. Assume:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the pilot estimates are consistent,
        $\norm{\hat\sigma-\sigma}_\infty+\norm{\hat\gamma_1-\gamma_1}_\infty+\norm{\hat q-q}_\infty\to0$ in
        probability, with $\sigma,\gamma_1$ bounded away from $0$ on $\cZ$;
  \item finite-pool feasibility, $\sup_z(n/N)\,\tilde p(z)/q(z)\le1-c$, so that weighted sampling
        without replacement can realize $\tilde p$ up to constants;
  \item the pool covers every window, $Nh^dc_q\gg\log N$.
\end{itemize}
Let $E_\epsilon$ be the event on which three things hold:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\norm{\hat q\hat A/(qA)-1}_\infty\le\epsilon$;
  \item $\hat\sigma/\sigma$ and $\hat\gamma_1/\gamma_1$ lie in $[\tfrac12,2]$;
  \item every bandwidth window contains $\gtrsim n\tilde p\hat h^d$ revealed points.
\end{itemize}
Let $\epsilon_{\mathrm{pool}}=\sup_z|\text{realized design density}/\tilde p-1|$ be the
without-replacement approximation error. Then \dfsp{} with the plug-in local bandwidth satisfies
\[
  R\;\lesssim\;\{(1-c_0)n\}^{-\alpha}\,(1-\eta)^{-\alpha}\,\frac{1+\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}\,
  (1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{pool}})^{-\alpha}\,J(p^{*})\;+\;\Pr(E_\epsilon^{c}).
\]
For binary labels the risk is at most $1$, which is why $\Pr(E_\epsilon^c)$ enters additively.
\end{target}

Two exact inequalities drive the displayed factors. First, $\tilde p\ge(1-\eta)\hat p$ gives
$J(\tilde p)\le(1-\eta)^{-\alpha}J(\hat p)$. Second, $\hat p$ minimizes the estimated surrogate
$\hat J$, and $\hat J\in[(1-\epsilon)J,(1+\epsilon)J]$ on $E_\epsilon$, so
$J(\hat p)\le\frac{1+\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}J(p^{*})$. The pool factor follows in the same way from a
realized density $\ge(1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{pool}})\tilde p$. What remains open is a rate for
$\Pr(E_\epsilon^c)$, which requires sup-norm consistency of $\hat\gamma_1$ at the scale $\rho$ above.

The corresponding uniform-design bound replaces $J(p^{*})$ by $J(q)$. The comparison
$J(p^{*})<J(q)$ is a statement about \emph{upper bounds}. It holds strictly iff
$\gamma_1^{2d}\sigma^{4\gamma_2}q^{-2\gamma_2}$ is not constant on $\{q>0\}$. It is not a proved
improvement in actual risk. Two things remain open: consistency of the local-complexity estimator
$\hat\gamma_1$, and whether $\eta=\eta_n\to0$ can recover the oracle constant. E3 is the empirical
test of whether the bound improvement shows up in actual risk.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
Random reveal; judge uncertainty (entropy of $\QJ$, or $|p_J-\tfrac12|$); disagreement-targeted
correction in the style of RLTHF \citep{xu2025rlthf}; Fisher-information design \citep{shen2025active};
epistemic-uncertainty targeting \citep{lail2026decomposing}; the $\sigma\sqrt q$ rule (global
bandwidth). Active labeling itself is not claimed as new. The contribution is a rule derived from the
distributional personalization risk under the population measure.
