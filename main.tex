\large Research proposal}

\begin{abstract}
Preference labels for post-training language models are often produced by an LLM judge instead of
by people. Judges are cheap but differ systematically from human raters, while human labels are
accurate but scarce. We study how to combine judge outputs on many comparisons with human labels on
a few, in order to estimate human preference probabilities and to train a model toward them.

Our starting point is the few-shot personalization method of \citet{li2026personalizing}, which
corrects a pretrained predictor using a small labeled sample. We apply it with three changes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the correction is estimated locally in a space of features of the judge's output
        distribution, not only its win probability;
  \item a sampling rule decides which comparisons should receive human labels;
  \item the corrected probabilities are used as targets for preference optimization.
\end{itemize}
The estimator reduces to a purely human estimator when the judge is unhelpful. We give conditions
under which the judge's full output distribution improves on its win probability, and a bound on
the post-trained model's preference risk in terms of the estimation error. All experiments reuse an
existing human-labeled dataset.
\end{abstract}
