\large Research proposal --- draft v0.4}
\author{LLM-humanchoice project}
\date{28 September 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
LLM judges are cheap, and under repeated sampling, logit elicitation or a structured rating
protocol they produce a \emph{distribution} over preference outcomes rather than a single winner.
That distribution is not the human preference distribution that post-training should target, and
human labels are scarce. We propose to treat the judge as a distribution-valued black-box predictor
and to \emph{personalize} it to humans with a few labels, extending the few-shot personalization
(FSP) framework of \citet{li2026personalizing} from scalar nonparametric regression to conditional
preference distributions. The proposal has three linked components: (I) a distributional FSP
estimator (\dfsp) that corrects the judge through a locally smoothed discrepancy and contains the
human-only estimator as a special case; (II) a label-acquisition rule derived from the estimator's
integrated risk; and (III) post-training of an open-weight policy against the personalized target
(\fsppo), with bounds that propagate the personalization error into the policy's excess
preference risk. Several short tool results are proved here: a nested-information decomposition
that defines when the judge distribution adds information beyond its scalar summary, an oracle
judge-budget bound, and bridges from personalization error to post-training excess risk (including
a fast rate under quadratic growth). The proposal rests on two things. The first is an empirical
question, answered with a calibrated test: does a graded, two-order judge distribution carry
human-relevant information beyond its win probability? The second is a label-acquisition rule
under the population risk, which improves constants but not rates. Minimax lower bounds for
bounded preference targets and an end-to-end corollary for post-training are supporting theory,
stated as targets. All experiments are retrospective on an existing human-labeled dataset, with a
common test set held out by prompt.
\end{abstract}
